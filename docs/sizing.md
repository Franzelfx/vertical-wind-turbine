## Overvoltage

The free-running rotor drives the bus past the charger's 24 V at
10.2 m/s, which happens whenever the pack is full, and reaches
71 V at 30 m/s. The relay trips at
22.5 V, below the charger's 26 V stop (ADR-006). Its coil path
draws 144 mW at a 12 V bus, about 2 % of what the rotor makes there.

## Stopping the rotor

The brake switch shorts the bus through 4.7 ohm. It only slows the rotor: at 10 m/s
it holds 817 rpm with 9.5 W in the generator, and the
generator's 12 W limit is reached at 10.9 m/s.
Free-running, the rotor reaches its assumed 3000 rpm limit at
24.1 m/s. **Lock it with the parking pin before a forecast
storm (ADR-005).**

## Vibration

The pier's first mode is 4.9 Hz, which is 296 rpm. The rotor
reaches that speed at 4.7 m/s at its best tip speed ratio, inside the everyday
range. With the assumed 500 g mm of imbalance the swing is
1.3 mm at resonance, small but continuous at the wind you will see most.
Balance the rotor (B4). If it shakes, guy wires move the mode to about
31 Hz, near the rotor's running range at high wind, so check
again then. Assumed: Q = 20.

## Power budget with no wind

0.55 mW from the pack: 76 Wh lasts
5772 days. Li-ion's own self-discharge is the larger term.

Charging stops outside 0-45 degC, by the NTC on
the pack (TS pin). In a German winter that means no charging on frosty days.
